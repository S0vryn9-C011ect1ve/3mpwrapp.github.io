## (a) Members share wins &amp; patterns

It starts with the **[Member Wins](/wins/)** hub. When a member wins a WSIB
appeal, secures an accommodation through HRTO, or gets an ODSP approval, they
can share *how* — anonymized, no names or figures required. Every shared story
is one more data point about what works and where the system breaks.

## (b) 3mpwrApp identifies systemic patterns

Shared stories are not opinion — they sit on top of the largest open analysis of
Ontario tribunal decisions we've built. The patterns members report in their
wins are checked against the patterns already visible in the data:

- **[Research hub — 230,000+ tribunal decisions analyzed](/research/)**
- **[Research data sources: WSIAT, HRTO, ONSBT, WSIB &amp; CanLII](/research-data-sources/)**
- **[How to use this data](/how-to-use-this-data/)**
- **[Tribunal data visualizations](/tribunal-visualizations/)**

The site's own research has already surfaced recurring, documentable patterns —
for example:

- **WSIAT outcomes over time** — [WSIAT outcome timeline 2020–2026](/wsiat-outcome-timeline-2020-2026.html)
- **HRTO outcomes over time** — [HRTO outcome timeline 2020–2026](/hrto-outcome-timeline-2020-2026.html)
- **Cross-tribunal comparison** — [who wins, and where](/cross-tribunal-success-rates.html)
- **How denial tactics evolve** — [WSIB appeal funnel](/wsib-appeal-funnel.html) and the [WSIB denial network](/wsib-denial-network-visualization.html)
- **Keyword patterns in the case law** — [CanLII keyword network](/connecting-the-dots-canlii-keyword-visualization-network.html) and [WSIAT keyword network](/connecting-the-dots-wsiat-keyword-network.html)
- **Which issues generate the most volume** — [tribunal navigator](/tribunal-navigator.html)

When members' lived experience lines up with what the data shows, a *pattern*
— not a anecdote — is what we act on.

## (c) Patterns become campaigns

Once a pattern is documented and repeated across members, it gets named and
organized. A campaign takes a systemic problem the evidence has exposed and
turns it into something a community can rally behind — with the receipts
attached. This is the step where the **[Campaigns](/campaigns/)** space lives:
grassroots, member-led, and built on the research above rather than slogans.

## (d) Campaigns drive policy change

Evidence-backed, collective campaigns are harder to dismiss than individual
complaints. When the pattern is clear, the data is open, and the people affected
are organized, the ask to decision-makers is concrete: *here is the problem,
here is the proof, here is the fix.* That is how member evidence becomes policy
pressure.

## The evidence base — and its limits

Everything on this page traces back to research the site has already published.
We cite our own work rather than assert outcomes we can't show:

| Claim | Where the evidence lives |
|---|---|
| WSIAT decisions show recurring outcome patterns | [WSIAT outcome timeline](/wsiat-outcome-timeline-2020-2026.html) · [research hub](/research/) |
| HRTO accommodation denials recur | [HRTO outcome timeline](/hrto-outcome-timeline-2020-2026.html) |
| Denial tactics are documented and evolving | [WSIB appeal funnel](/wsib-appeal-funnel.html) · [WSIB denial network](/wsib-denial-network-visualization.html) |
| Legal precedent patterns are traceable | [CanLII keyword network](/connecting-the-dots-canlii-keyword-visualization-network.html) |
| Data is open and checkable | [Research data sources](/research-data-sources/) · [How to use this data](/how-to-use-this-data/) |

We do **not** claim a campaign has changed a specific law unless that change is
documented. Accuracy is the point — a movement built on shaky numbers loses the
one thing that makes it powerful: proof.
