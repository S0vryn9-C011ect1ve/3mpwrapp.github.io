Veterans Benefits and Services in Canada (VAC)

Scope note. Veterans who sustain a service-related injury or illness may qualify for Veterans Affairs Canada (VAC) benefits instead of, or in addition to, workers’ compensation under WSIB (or the provincial equivalent). The two systems are separate, with different eligibility tests, evidence rules, and appeal routes. Many injured workers — and veterans — never learn that a second system exists.

Verification status. Every program below was read from veterans.gc.ca on 2026-10-03. Programs and rates change; confirm against the source before relying on a figure.

Who can apply

You may qualify for a VAC disability benefit if you are:

  • a Canadian Armed Forces member or Veteran
  • a current or former member of the RCMP
  • a Second World War or Korean War Veteran (includes Merchant Navy)
  • certain civilians who served in the Second World War

You should apply if you:

  1. have a diagnosed medical condition or disability, and
  2. can show the condition is related to your service.

Two-part test. Both parts. A condition alone is not enough; service connection alone is not enough.

Gulf War Illness is now recognized by VAC as a valid medical diagnosis. Veterans with that diagnosis are encouraged to apply.

The two disability benefit options

A disability benefit is a tax-free financial payment. The amount depends on how strongly your condition is related to service (entitlement) and how severe it is including its impact on quality of life (assessment).

You receive either:

Benefit What it is
Pain and Suffering Compensation Lifetime monthly benefit or lump sum — your choice
Disability Pension Lifetime monthly benefit, increased if you have dependents (spouse, common-law partner, and/or children)

Important eligibility limits on the Disability Pension

  • Provided if you served with the CAF in the Second World War or Korean War
  • For other CAF service, only if your application was made before 1 April 2006

If you are a modern-era veteran (post-2006), Pain and Suffering Compensation is typically the applicable route. Confirm current rules before advising anyone.

Related programs

Program What it provides
Additional Pain and Suffering Compensation Monthly payments for severe, permanent service-related disability that creates a barrier to life after service
Critical injury benefit One-time payment recognising the immediate impact of the most severe, traumatic service-related injuries or diseases
Treatment Benefits Coverage for medical and health-related services, prescriptions and devices
Rehabilitation services Services to improve health and adjust to life after service
Clothing allowance Monthly payments if you need new or special clothing due to health issues
Benefits for survivors Financial support or compensation for survivors of a disability pensioner, or of a member/Veteran who died in service or of a service-related illness or injury
Financial advice Up to $500 toward professional advice if you received a lump sum >5% of the current maximum
Telemedicine service Free virtual health care for Veterans and their families who have no primary healthcare provider
Guided support See below

Programs tied specifically to a Disability Pension

  • Attendance allowance — monthly payments for a pensioner whose health needs require daily personal care support
  • Exceptional incapacity allowance — monthly payments if the illness or injury impacts your quality of life

Disability Pension corrective payment

If you received a VAC benefit between 2003 and 2010 under the Pension Act, you may be owed a corrective payment due to a calculation discrepancy. Most are automatic. Contact VAC if you no longer receive a benefit, or you represent the estate of a deceased recipient. Legally entitled inheritors can apply.

Guided support — the navigation service

Optional. You are assigned a Veteran Service Agent for up to 12 months to help identify your needs, access VAC benefits and services, and connect you with community resources.

Who qualifies — you must be living in Canada and:

  • be a former member of the CAF or the RCMP

and either:

  • need significant assistance coordinating services, with limited support, or
  • have recently completed case management and need help transitioning to self-service

Who does NOT qualify: spouses, survivors, and Veterans living outside Canada.

How to apply: there is no application form. Contact VAC directly.

Useful if you are:

  • struggling to navigate or complete the steps for multiple VAC benefits
  • arranging long-term care or significant equipment/home adaptations after a major decline
  • preparing to leave hospital or needing palliative care coordination
  • concluding case management and moving to self-service
  • facing a major life change, such as a move, that makes supports hard to access

Related: Case Management (a Case Manager helps set goals and build a plan for independence), and the Transition Interview (meet with a VAC employee before release from service).

How to apply

Online — via My VAC Account, which uses a guided form (easier than the paper route).

Mail or in person — download the form (PEN-923, Disability Benefits First Application), then drop it at any VAC office, CAF Transition Centre, or Service Canada office. Or mail it to the address on the form.

Free help with your application:

  • VAC office, CAF Transition Centre, or Service Canada staff
  • Service Officers with The Royal Canadian Legion — free assistance
  • The War Amps of Canada — free assistance

None of that costs the veteran anything.

Reviews, appeals, reassessment

Appeal — if you disagree with a decision, you may request a Departmental review in writing. This is a different route from a WSIB appeal.

Reassessment — if the condition you are receiving benefits for worsens and medical evidence can show the change, request a reassessment. If confirmed, your benefit is adjusted unless you are already at maximum.

How VAC reviews your claim

Eligibility Entitlement Guidelines — current medical and scientific descriptions of known service-related injuries and diseases.

Table of Disabilities — helps the adjudicator assess level of impairment and impact on quality of life.

Medical questionnaires — support diagnosis and supplement the Table of Disabilities.

Claim Summary Tool — an AI-enabled tool VAC uses to support review of applications and help Veterans receive decisions sooner.

Disability Tax Credit (DTC)

The DTC reduces income tax for people with physical or mental impairments, or their supporting family members. It is non-refundable and administered by the Canada Revenue Agency, not VAC. CRA — About the disability tax credit

VAC vs WSIB / ODSP — why this distinction matters

  WSIB / provincial WC VAC
Trigger Work-related injury or illness Service-related injury or illness
Tests causation Employment + activity Service connection + diagnosed condition
Appeals Internal tribunal (WSIAT, HRTO, etc.) Departmental review
Who pays Employer-funded Federal

A veteran injured at work may have both a WSIB claim and a VAC claim. They are not substitutes — and a denial from one system says nothing about the other.

The gap this article exists to close: injured workers are routinely told about workers’ compensation and disability programs, but almost never told that a parallel federal veterans system exists and may apply.
