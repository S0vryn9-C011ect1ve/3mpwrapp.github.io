Disability Programs When the Injury Wasn’t Work-Related

If you have a disability that was not caused by your job and not by military service, this page is for you. Many people assume that if they cannot get workers’ compensation, they have no options. That is not true — there are several separate federal and provincial programs, and they can be combined.

Verified 2026-10-03 against official government sources, linked throughout. Amounts and rules change — confirm before acting. Not legal advice.

First: the Canada Disability Benefit (CDB)

This is new, it is in force, and most people do not know it exists.

The Canada Disability Benefit began paying in July 2025. It is a federal benefit for people with a severe and prolonged disability, designed to top up — not replace — provincial supports.

  • Maximum: $204.20/month (July 2026 – June 2027), based on 2025 adjusted family net income. Adjusted for inflation annually; never decreases when the cost of living falls. (amount)
  • Ages 18–64. Under 18 you may apply up to 6 months before your 18th birthday, but nothing is paid until you turn 18. At 65+, back payments may be available for up to 24 months from application.
  • ⚠️ You must already be approved for the Disability Tax Credit. DTC approval is a hard prerequisite — no DTC, no CDB. This is the single most common reason a CDB application fails.
  • You must have filed your 2025 federal tax return to receive payments.
  • Back payments: up to 24 months from when ESDC receives your application, and never for months before June 2025.
  • $150 supplemental payment — a one-time lump sum to offset the cost of getting DTC certification, payable from 17 September 2026 to anyone who received a CDB payment between July 2025 and June 2026. One amount per approved DTC certificate. No application needed.
  • Decision timeline: 28 calendar days; 49 if a legal representative applies.
  • Apply: Service Canada mails a 6-digit application code. Online, or paper form CDB0004 (self) / CDB0005 (legal representative). (apply)
  • Full CDB page

The Disability Tax Credit (DTC) — the gateway

The DTC is a non-refundable federal tax credit administered by the Canada Revenue Agency. Practically, it is the gateway to the CDB.

  • A medical practitioner certifies a severe and prolonged impairment in one category, significant limitations in two or more, or therapy to support a vital function.
  • Marked restriction test: you cannot do the activity, or it takes 3× longer than someone of similar age without the impairment — even with therapy, medication and devices — present all or almost all the time (generally 90%+) and lasting at least 12 months.
  • Nine categories: walking, mental functions, dressing, feeding, eliminating, hearing, speaking, vision, life-sustaining therapy.
  • Who may certify: physician, nurse practitioner, optometrist (vision), audiologist (hearing), occupational therapist (walking/feeding/dressing), physiotherapist (walking), psychologist (mental functions), speech-language pathologist (speaking).
  • ⚠️ Choose ONE application method. Part A and Part B must be in the same format or the application will not be processed. Applying at the same time as your tax return can delay assessment — submit the DTC first.
  • If the practitioner charges a fee, you pay it (it may be claimable as a medical expense). This is exactly what the $150 CDB payment offsets. (eligibility)
  • ⚠️ UNVERIFIED: no dedicated canada.ca page was found for appealing a DTC refusal specifically. Ask for your Notice of Objection with the determination letter and follow the CRA objection process.

Ontario Disability Support Program (ODSP) — Ontario only

  • Eligibility — all five: 18 or older · Ontario resident · assets within program limits · in financial need · meets the definition of person with a disability or is a member of a prescribed class. Under-18s may apply up to 6 months before turning 18.
  • Pays: monthly income support — basic needs (food, clothing, personal items) plus a shelter allowance (rent/mortgage, heat, hydro, water, property tax, insurance, condo fees). Also a Board and Lodging amount and a Remote Communities Allowance (Northern Ontario, north of the 50th parallel, no year-round road access).
  • Maximum up to $1,436/month for a single person (basic needs + shelter), after the 1.9% increase effective 1 July 2026 — the fifth increase since Sept 2022 (>22% cumulative).
  • Also provides: health benefits including prescription drugs and vision care.
  • ✅ The Canada Disability Benefit is exempt as income — receiving CDB does not reduce your ODSP amount or eligibility.
  • Apply: apply.mybenefits.mccss.gov.on.ca
  • Appeals, two stages: (1) internal review within 30 days; (2) Social Benefits Tribunal. Interim assistance (financial aid while waiting) can be requested if the appeal is causing hardship and you were already receiving ODSP/Ontario Works — not available if appealing an initial application. (appeals)
  • ODSP

Alberta AISH — Alberta only

Assured Income for the Severely Handicapped — for people whose severe disability permanently prevents employment, where all relevant treatment is complete and no improvement is expected.

  • Also 18+, not eligible for OAS, living in Alberta, Canadian citizen or PR, not in a correctional or designated mental health facility.
  • Assets: non-exempt assets may not total more than $100,000. Your own CPP-D, EI, WCB and CDB income counts (deducted dollar-for-dollar) — a spouse’s is only partially exempt. You must apply for CPP-D, CDB, EI and WCB.
  • Provides: monthly living allowance (modified if in an approved Continuing Care Home), monthly child benefit, a Health Benefits Card (prescription drugs, basic dental, optical — one eye exam and one pair of glasses every 2 years for adults, diabetes supplies, ambulance, AADL), and personal benefits (special diets, medical supplies, moving, funeral arrangements, remote-community home maintenance).
  • Apply: dia.alberta.ca/main
  • Appeal: Citizen’s Appeal Panel within 30 days. The minister may extend the deadline for delay. Judicial review is in the Court of King’s Bench via Form 7 within 6 months. The Panel cannot review the maximum living allowance value or a denial for failing to apply for CPP-D/OAS.
  • AISH · appeal

CPP Disability Benefits — Canada-wide

  • All three must be met: age 18–65 · a mental or physical disability that stops you working at any job on a regular basis and is long-term · contributions in 4 of the 6 years before disability stopped you working (or 3 of those 6 years plus 25 years of total contributions).
  • Maximum $1,741.20/month (2026); average for new beneficiaries July 2026 $1,236.32/month.
  • Working thresholds (2026): under $7,400 earned — no effect. Between $7,400 and $20,971.45 — may affect benefits. At or above $20,971.45 — likely no longer eligible. Contact Service Canada when you reach these amounts.
  • Advocacy note: a list of 40 “grave conditions” (ALS, Parkinson’s, schizophrenia, MS, various cancers, quadriplegia and others) has a high probability of meeting eligibility — useful context, not automatic approval.
  • If you already get the CPP retirement pension you may qualify for CPP Post-Retirement Disability Benefits, considered automatically.
  • CPP disability

EI Sickness Benefits — Canada-wide

Short-term only — for a temporary inability to work.

  • Must demonstrate: unable to work for medical reasons · regular weekly earnings dropped more than 40% for at least one week · 600 insured hours in the 52 weeks before your claim started.
  • Pays: up to 26 weeks at 55% of earnings, maximum $729/week.
  • Medical certificate required (form INS5140 or the practitioner’s own). Signing professionals include doctor, chiropractor, podiatrist, optometrist, psychologist, dentist, midwife, nurse practitioner. Keep the certificate for 6 years.
  • ⚠️ Boundary: for long-term or permanent disability, EI points you to CPP disability and Quebec Pension Plan disability.
  • EI sickness

⚠️ Three deadlines that are NOT the same

Getting this wrong forfeits an appeal. They are not interchangeable:

Program Reconsideration deadline
CPP disability 90 days
Canada Disability Benefit 180 days
EI 30 days

CPP, two stages: request reconsideration to Service Canada within 90 days (online, form ISP-1145 for disability / ISP-1238 general, or a signed written request) → appeal to the Social Security Tribunal (SST) within 90 days of that decision. To have a representative at the SST you must give the Tribunal their details; to let them also communicate with Service Canada, complete ISP-1603 separately.

CDB: reconsideration within 180 days (form CDB0006) → SST.

EI: reconsideration form INS5210 within 30 days, no fee. Submit any new information to Service Canada first — they may change the original decision without a full reconsideration.

⚠️ Changed 1 April 2026: the EI Benefits Appeal Tribunal (BOA) has replaced the Social Security Tribunal General Division for EI reconsideration appeals. Content saying “appeal EI to the SST” is out of date for EI specifically.

Two things that do NOT exist

So you don’t waste time applying to them:

  • There is no federal program called “Participib.” No such benefit was found on canada.ca. You may mean the Canada Workers Benefit (CWB) — a different program, claimed on your tax return at line 45300, and it is a tax credit, not a disability benefit.
  • “Working for Workers” acts are Ontario employment-standards legislation, not disability benefits. They create no disability benefit.

How these combine

You do not have to choose one:

  • CDB + ODSP/AISH — CDB is exempt as income for ODSP. For AISH, your CDB is counted, so report the decision to AISH.
  • CPP-D + ODSP — ask how they interact for your circumstances; they are separate.
  • DTC + CDB — DTC approval is the prerequisite, and the $150 payment offsets the cost of getting it.
  • DTC + any income support — the credit applies regardless of which program pays you.
