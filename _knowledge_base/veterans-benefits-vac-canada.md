# Veterans Benefits and Services in Canada (VAC)

> **Scope note.** Veterans who sustain a service-related injury or illness may qualify
> for **Veterans Affairs Canada (VAC)** benefits *instead of, or in addition to,*
> workers' compensation under **WSIB** (or the provincial equivalent). The two systems
> are separate, with different eligibility tests, evidence rules, and appeal routes.
> Many injured workers — and veterans — never learn that a second system exists.

> **Verification status.** Every program below was read from veterans.gc.ca, vrab-tacra.gc.ca,
> laws.justice.gc.ca or canada.ca on **2026-10-07**. Programs and rates change; confirm
> against the source before relying on a figure.

---

## Who can apply

You may qualify for a VAC disability benefit if you are:

- a Canadian Armed Forces member or Veteran
- a current or former member of the **RCMP**
- a Second World War or Korean War Veteran (**includes Merchant Navy**)
- certain **civilians** who served in the Second World War

You should apply if you:

1. have a **diagnosed** medical condition or disability, **and**
2. can show the condition is **related to your service**.

Two-part test. Both parts. A condition alone is not enough; service connection alone
is not enough.

**Gulf War Illness** is now recognized by VAC as a valid medical diagnosis. Veterans
with that diagnosis are encouraged to apply.

## The two statutory schemes: Pension Act vs Veterans Well-being Act

This is the part of the veterans system people most often get wrong.

### The older scheme — the Pension Act

The **Pension Act** (R.S.C., 1985, c. P-6) is the historic lifetime-pension scheme.
Under it, a service-related disability is paid as a **Disability Pension**: a lifetime
**monthly** payment that increases if you have dependants (spouse, common-law partner,
and/or children).

A Disability Pension under the Pension Act is available if:

- you served with the CAF in the **Second World War or Korean War**, **or**
- for other CAF service, your application for disability benefits was made **before 1 April 2006**.

### The modern scheme — the Veterans Well-being Act (the "New Veterans Charter")

The benefits introduced in 2006 as the **New Veterans Charter** are now consolidated in
the **Veterans Well-being Act** (cited as **c-16.8** on the Justice Laws website — *not*
the VRAB Act; the Veterans Review and Appeal Board Act is a separate Act at citation
**v-1.6**). For most modern-era veterans (service after 2006), this is the scheme that applies.

Its main disability benefit is **Pain and Suffering Compensation (PSC)** — a tax-free
payment for the impact a service-related condition has on your life. You choose between:

- a **lifetime monthly** payment, **or**
- a **lump sum** — your choice.

### At a glance

| | **Disability Pension (Pension Act)** | **Pain and Suffering Compensation (Veterans Well-being Act)** |
|---|---|---|
| Payment | Lifetime monthly | Monthly **or** lump sum (your choice) |
| Dependants | Increased if you have dependants | No dependant supplement |
| Who it's for | War veterans; pre-1-Apr-2006 CAF applicants | Modern-era veterans (post-2006 service) |
| Tax treatment | Tax-free | Tax-free |

A disability benefit is a **tax-free** financial payment. The amount depends on
how strongly your condition is **related to service** (*entitlement*) and how severe
it is including its impact on quality of life (*assessment*).

You receive **either** Pain and Suffering Compensation or a Disability Pension.

### Important eligibility limits on the Disability Pension

- Provided if you served with the CAF in the **Second World War or Korean War**
- For **other** CAF service, only if your application was made **before 1 April 2006**

If you are a modern-era veteran (post-2006), Pain and Suffering Compensation is
typically the applicable route. Confirm current rules before advising anyone.

## Related programs

| Program | What it provides |
|---|---|
| **Additional Pain and Suffering Compensation** | Monthly payments for severe, permanent service-related disability that creates a barrier to life after service |
| **Critical injury benefit** | One-time payment recognising the immediate impact of the most severe, traumatic service-related injuries or diseases |
| **Treatment Benefits** | Coverage for medical and health-related services, prescriptions and devices |
| **Rehabilitation services** | Services to improve health and adjust to life after service |
| **Clothing allowance** | Monthly payments if you need new or special clothing due to health issues |
| **Benefits for survivors** | Financial support or compensation for survivors of a disability pensioner, or of a member/Veteran who died in service or of a service-related illness or injury |
| **Financial advice** | Up to **$500** toward professional advice if you received a lump sum **>5% of the current maximum** |
| **Telemedicine service** | Free virtual health care for Veterans **and their families** who have no primary healthcare provider |
| **Guided support** | See below |

### Programs tied specifically to a Disability Pension

- **Attendance allowance** — monthly payments for a pensioner whose health needs require daily personal care support
- **Exceptional incapacity allowance** — monthly payments if the illness or injury impacts your quality of life

### Disability Pension corrective payment

If you received a VAC benefit **between 2003 and 2010** under the Pension Act, you
may be owed a **corrective payment** due to a calculation discrepancy. Most are
automatic. Contact VAC if you no longer receive a benefit, or you represent the
estate of a deceased recipient. Legally entitled inheritors can apply.

## The Assistance Service (free, 24/7 counselling)

The **VAC Assistance Service** provides **free and confidential psychological support**,
available **24 hours a day, 365 days a year**, for Veterans, former RCMP members,
their family members, and caregivers. It is separate from the disability-benefits application.

## Career transition programs

### Career Transition Services (CTS)

One-on-one help with career counselling, resume writing, interview preparation and
job searching, delivered by VAC's national service provider (**Agilec**).

**Who qualifies:**

- a still-serving CAF member (Regular or Reserve Force) who completed basic training,
- a former member who completed basic training and was released from the CAF **on or after 1 April 2006**, or who is entitled to the Canadian Forces Income Support Benefit,
- a spouse, common-law partner, or survivor of an eligible Veteran (same 1 April 2006 rule).

If you are in VAC's **vocational rehabilitation**, you do **not** qualify for CTS
(career support comes through that service instead). VAC aims to decide within
**four weeks**. You can use CTS more than once in your lifetime. Apply via My VAC Account
or by mail (VAC-1533 for Veterans/Members; VAC-1535 for spouses/common-law/survivors).

### Education and Training Benefit

Financial support to help you pursue education and training that supports your career goals.

### Vocational Rehabilitation (for injured Veterans)

For Veterans whose injury or illness primarily resulted from service and who need help
returning to work or finding new work.

## Trauma and injury benefits

| Benefit | What it covers |
|---|---|
| **Critical Injury Benefit** | A **one-time** payment for the most severe, traumatic service-related injuries or diseases |
| **Pain and Suffering Compensation / Disability Award** | Tax-free recognition for the lasting impact of a diagnosed service-related condition (PTSD, hearing loss, tinnitus, Gulf War Illness, etc.) |
| **Treatment Benefits** | Medical and health-related services, prescriptions and devices |
| **Rehabilitation services** | Help to improve health and adjust to life after service |
| **VAC Assistance Service** | Free, confidential 24/7 psychological support |

## Guided support — the navigation service

**Optional.** You are assigned a **Veteran Service Agent** for **up to 12 months**
to help identify your needs, access VAC benefits and services, and connect you with
community resources.

**Who qualifies** — you must be living in Canada **and**:

- be a former member of the CAF or the RCMP

**and** either:

- need significant assistance coordinating services, with limited support, **or**
- have recently completed case management and need help transitioning to self-service

**Who does NOT qualify:** spouses, survivors, and Veterans living outside Canada.

**How to apply:** **there is no application form.** Contact VAC directly.

Useful if you are:

- struggling to navigate or complete the steps for multiple VAC benefits
- arranging long-term care or significant equipment/home adaptations after a major decline
- preparing to leave hospital or needing palliative care coordination
- concluding case management and moving to self-service
- facing a major life change, such as a move, that makes supports hard to access

Related: **Case Management** (a Case Manager helps set goals and build a plan for
independence), and the **Transition Interview** (meet with a VAC employee before
release from service).

## How to apply

**Online** — via **My VAC Account**, which uses a guided form (easier than the
paper route).

**Mail or in person** — download the form (PEN-923, *Disability Benefits First
Application*), then drop it at any VAC office, CAF Transition Centre, or Service
Canada office. Or mail it to the address on the form.

**Free help with your application:**
- VAC office, CAF Transition Centre, or Service Canada staff
- **Service Officers with The Royal Canadian Legion** — free assistance
- **The War Amps of Canada** — free assistance

None of that costs the veteran anything.

## Reviews, appeals, reassessment — the VRAB process

If you disagree with a VAC decision on a disability benefit, you have a right to review or appeal.

**Step 1 — Departmental review (within VAC).** Request a written departmental review
and submit **new evidence** not in your original application. A VAC adjudicator
re-examines your submission and decides whether to issue a new decision. This is a
different route from a WSIB appeal.

**Step 2 — Veterans Review and Appeal Board (VRAB).** An **independent federal tribunal**,
separate from VAC, offering **two levels of appeal**:

1. **Review Hearing** — heard by **two** Board Members across Canada (or by video/teleconference);
   **non-adversarial**; you are encouraged to attend and testify; travel may be reimbursed;
   hearings last about **30 minutes**; goal is a written decision within **six weeks**.
2. **Appeal Hearing** — heard by **three** Board Members (not involved in your review hearing),
   usually in **Charlottetown**; you are **not required to attend**; the decision is
   **final and binding**; goal is a written decision within **six weeks**.

VRAB's overall service standard is to **hear and decide within 16 weeks** once your
representative says the case is ready. VRAB can hear PSC, Disability Pension, Disability
Award, Critical Injury Benefit, special awards (Additional P&SC, Attendance Allowance,
Exceptional Incapacity Allowance, Clothing Allowance), the Compassionate Award, and
final War Veterans Allowance appeals.

**Reconsideration.** If new, credible evidence emerges, or an error in fact or law is
found, you can apply to VRAB to reopen the case (not routine).

**Judicial review.** The final remaining avenue is a **judicial review at the Federal
Court of Canada**, available only in specific circumstances (error of law/fact or
significant overlooked facts). You have **30 days** to apply. BPA lawyers are **not**
mandated to represent you at the Federal Court.

**Free help to appeal:** the **Bureau of Pensions Advocates** (free legal advice and
representation within VAC), **Royal Canadian Legion Service Officers** (free), or a
private representative at your own cost. VRAB decisions are public (depersonalized)
and published on CanLII.

**Reassessment** — if the condition you are receiving benefits for **worsens** and
medical evidence can show the change, request a reassessment. If confirmed, your
benefit is adjusted unless you are already at maximum.

## How VAC reviews your claim

**Eligibility Entitlement Guidelines** — current medical and scientific descriptions
of known service-related injuries and diseases.

**Table of Disabilities** — helps the adjudicator assess level of impairment and
impact on quality of life.

**Medical questionnaires** — support diagnosis and supplement the Table of Disabilities.

**Claim Summary Tool** — an **AI-enabled** tool VAC uses to support review of
applications and help Veterans receive decisions sooner.

## Disability Tax Credit (DTC)

The DTC reduces income tax for people with physical or mental impairments, or their
supporting family members. It is **non-refundable** and administered by the **Canada
Revenue Agency**, not VAC. [CRA — About the disability tax credit](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/segments/tax-credits-deductions-persons-disabilities/disability-tax-credit/about-dtc.html)

## VAC vs WSIB / ODSP — why this distinction matters

| | **WSIB / provincial WC** | **VAC** |
|---|---|---|
| Trigger | Work-related injury or illness | **Service-related** injury or illness |
| Tests causation | Employment + activity | Service connection + diagnosed condition |
| Appeals | Internal tribunal (WSIAT, HRTO, etc.) | Departmental review → VRAB (independent tribunal) |
| Who pays | Employer-funded | Federal |

A veteran injured at work may have **both** a WSIB claim and a VAC claim. They are
not substitutes — and a denial from one system says nothing about the other.

**The gap this article exists to close:** injured workers are routinely told about
workers' compensation and disability programs, but almost never told that a parallel
federal veterans system exists and may apply.
