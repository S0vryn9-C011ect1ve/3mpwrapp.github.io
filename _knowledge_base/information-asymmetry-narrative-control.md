# Who Tells Your Story? The Information Asymmetry Behind WSIB

**Reviewed 2026-10-03.** This article documents *who gets to narrate* the WSIB system —
not the amounts. If you want the numbers, see
[WSIB Premium Rebates](/knowledge-base/wsib-premium-rebates/).

> **What this is:** a review of what a person searching for information about WSIB
> actually finds, and a record of the worker-side sources that exist.
> **What this is not:** not legal advice, and not a claim that any specific source
> published anything untrue. It is a claim about **who is present.**

---

## The finding

**Search results for WSIB topics are overwhelmingly written by the parties the system
benefits.** Employer-side, government, and legal-advisory content dominates. Injured
workers themselves appear mainly as **statistics** — as denominators in a "safety success"
story — rather than as people describing their own experience.

This is not evidence of conspiracy. It is a structural observation about who has the
resources to publish, rank, and maintain a web presence.

### Why this matters more than it sounds

The Meredith Principles (1913) describe a system built on three ideas: **no-fault**,
**collective liability**, and **security of income**. The intent was that the system
serves the injured worker.

But a no-fault, collectively-funded system still requires *someone to explain it to
you*. And the people with the most capacity to explain it are:

- the **board** that administers it,
- the **employers** it funds,
- the **law firms** that advise on it,
- the **employer-side trade press** that covers it.

None of these are lying. But the result is a public record in which the injured
worker's own account is structurally scarce.

## What is actually on the worker side

The worker-side record exists — it is just **harder to find and less indexed**. The
sources that came out of this review:

| Source | What it contributes |
|---|---|
| **Injured Workers Community Legal Clinic (IWC)** — via [Rank & File](https://www.rankandfile.ca/wsib-special-report/) | FOI-based analysis of under-compensation during the Marshall Years |
| [CUPE Ontario](https://cupe.on.ca/2-billion-in-wsib-rebates-a-gift-to-employers-an-insult-to-workers/) | Union analysis of rebates flowing to OHSA-convicted employers |
| **Canadian Association of Physically Injured Against Disability (CAWAA)** | [Meredith Principles history](https://cawaa.org/pages/the-meredith-principles) |
| [Socialist Project](https://www.socialistproject.ca/) | Analysis framing the rebate pool as a "slush fund" |
| **Safety Mag** (2008) | The claim-process hardship piece — still one of the clearest worker-side explanations of process |
| **r/OntarioWSIB**, **r/ontario** | Raw community voice — people describing denials in their own words |
| **First-person accounts** | The most valuable primary source in the entire dossier |

**The pattern:** the worker-side record is produced by **legal clinics, unions, and
people writing their own experience** — organizations with mission and members, but
without marketing budgets, SEO, or newsrooms.

---

## What this means for you

**1. Absence of evidence is not evidence of absence.** If you searched and found little
worker-side material, that reflects who publishes, not whether claimants are affected.

**2. The absence of worker voices is itself a finding.** A system that spends billions
and generates hundreds of thousands of decisions, in which the people it exists for
barely appear in the public record, is telling you something about where power sits.

**3. When you write to WSIB, to an employer, or to a tribunal, you are filling a gap.**
The record is not balanced. Your account is not one voice among many equally-heard
ones — it is one of very few worker-side voices in a field dominated by the other side.
