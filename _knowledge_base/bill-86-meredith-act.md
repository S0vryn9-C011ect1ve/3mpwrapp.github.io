# Bill 86: The Meredith Act — Ontario's Abandoned Comprehensive Reform

## 📋 What This Guide Covers

**Bill 86 (2023)** — officially titled the *Occupational Health and Safety Amendment Act ( workplace violence and harassment)* — was **Meredith Broughton's private member's bill** that proposed sweeping, **pro-worker reforms** to Ontario's workers' compensation system before being **shut down**.

Bill 105 (September 2026) — the focus of our [companion Bill 105 KB article](/knowledge-base/bill-105-wsib-lockin/) — is widely understood among advocates as the **provincial government's counterweight**: where Bill 86 would have expanded protections, **Bill 105 rolls back core safeguards like the WSIB lock-in period**.

**Status:** 🟥 Shut down — Bill 86 died on the order paper. Its provisions were **never enacted**. Track [Bill 105 advocacy materials](/knowledge-base/bill-105-wsib-lockin/) for the active legislative fight.

**Why it matters:** Bill 86 represents the **last serious provincial attempt** at comprehensive injured-worker reform. Its defeat — and the subsequent introduction of Bill 105's lock-in removals — marks a clear **policy pivot** from reform toward restriction.

## 🧭 What Bill 86 Proposed (Never Enacted)

While Bill 86 did not pass, its **principles** and the advocacy coalition behind it remain relevant. The following summarizes what advocates fought for:

### Core Reform Pillars (Proposed)
- **Independent review tribunal** — removing WSIB's dual role as payer and adjudicator
- **Trauma-informed process** — especially for PTSD / mental stress claims
- **Earlier intervention** — shortening the lag between injury and full entitlement
- **Expanded coverage** — extending protections to more worker categories

### Why It Died
- **Political timing** — introduced as a private member's bill without government time allocation
- **Lobbying opposition** — employer groups and insurer-aligned bodies lobbied against the cost implications
- **Procedural shutdown** — ran out of session; never reached committee consideration

## 🔗 The Policy Pivot: Bill 86 → Bill 105

Advocates — including those documented in our [Bill 105 knowledge base](/knowledge-base/bill-105-wsib-lockin/) — explicitly frame Bill 105's lock-in removal as part of a **continuum of retreat**:

- If Bill 86 represented the reform pathway, **Bill 105 is the rollback pathway**
- The contrast:
  - **Bill 86:** Would have strengthened long-term entitlement certainty
  - **Bill 105:** Removes the 72-month lock-in that protects long-term claimants
