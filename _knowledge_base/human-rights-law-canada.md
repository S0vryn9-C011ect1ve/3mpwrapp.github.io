# Human Rights & Disability Law in Canada

How disability discrimination is prohibited and remedied in Canada — the two main tribunal
tracks (HRTO and CHRT), the protected grounds, the UNCRPD backdrop, and what a win looks like.

## Two tribunal tracks

| | **HRTO** (Ontario) | **CHRT** (federal) |
|---|---|---|
| Law | *Ontario Human Rights Code* | *Canadian Human Rights Act* |
| Coverage | Most Ontario employers, housing, services, unions | Federal govt, banks, telecom, transport, First Nations |
| File with | HRTO directly (Form 1) | **CHRC first**, then referral to CHRT |
| Source | [tribunalsontario.ca/hrto](https://www.tribunalsontario.ca/hrto/) | [chrt-tcdp.gc.ca](https://www.chrt-tcdp.gc.ca/) |

## Protected grounds — disability

- **Ontario *Human Rights Code*:** "disability" includes physical, mental, developmental,
  learning and mental disorders, injuries, illnesses, and previous/perceived disability.
  Broad duty to accommodate to **undue hardship**. [Code (SO 1990, c. H.19)](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90h19)
- **Federal *Canadian Human Rights Act* (s. 3):** disability **and** genetic characteristics
  are protected; conviction record (with pardon/suspension) is also protected federally.
  [Act s. 3](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/h-6/section-3.html)

Other grounds (both): race, colour, ancestry/origin, religion/creed, age, sex (pregnancy,
breastfeeding, gender identity/expression), sexual orientation, marital status, family
status. (Ontario adds citizenship, receipt of public assistance in housing, and record of
offences in employment; see [OHRC](https://www.ohrc.on.ca/).)

## The UNCRPD backdrop

Canada ratified the **Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities** in **2010**
and acceded to its **Optional Protocol** in **2019**. Key articles to cite:

- **Art 5** — equality and non-discrimination + reasonable accommodation as a duty
- **Art 9** — accessibility
- **Art 12** — equal recognition before the law
- **Art 19** — independent living and community inclusion
- **Art 27** — work and employment
- **Art 28** — adequate standard of living and social protection
- **Art 33** — national implementation/monitoring · **Art 34** — the CRPD Committee

The CRPD is international law; it shapes — but does not replace — the Code and the Act.
Through the Optional Protocol, after domestic remedies are exhausted, an individual
communication may go to the **CRPD Committee**. [UN CRPD](https://www.un.org/development/desa/disabilities/convention-on-the-rights-of-persons-with-disabilities.html)

## Remedies

Both tribunals focus on making the complainant whole and stopping the conduct:

- Compensation for lost wages
- Compensation for **injury to dignity, feelings and self-respect** (the standard award)
- Orders to stop the practice and to accommodate
- Policy/training changes

They generally do **not** award punitive damages.

## Deadlines & help

- **HRTO:** file within **one year** of the last incident (strict). Free.
  [Human Rights Legal Support Centre](https://hrlsc.on.ca/) gives free advice.
- **CHRC/CHRT:** file a complaint with the [Canadian Human Rights Commission](https://www.chrc-ccdp.gc.ca/).
