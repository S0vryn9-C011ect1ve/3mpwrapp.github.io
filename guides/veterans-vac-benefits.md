# Veterans Benefits & Appeals (VAC): Canada-Wide Guide

*Source-based guide. Every program, deadline and figure below was read from an official Government of Canada source (Veterans Affairs Canada, the Veterans Review and Appeal Board, Justice Laws, or Canada.ca) on 2026-10-07. Programs and rates change — confirm any figure against the source linked on the page before relying on it.*

> **Scope note.** If your injury or illness is related to **military service**, you may qualify for **Veterans Affairs Canada (VAC)** benefits — a separate federal system from provincial workers' compensation (WSIB and its provincial equivalents), ODSP, and CPP disability. A denial in one system says nothing about the other. Many veterans and injured workers never learn the federal veterans system exists. This guide explains it.

---

## Who can apply

You may qualify for a VAC disability benefit if you are:

- a **Canadian Armed Forces (CAF)** member or Veteran,
- a current or former member of the **Royal Canadian Mounted Police (RCMP)**,
- a **Second World War or Korean War** Veteran (**includes Merchant Navy**), or
- **certain civilians** who served in the Second World War.

**Two-part eligibility test.** A benefit requires both:

1. a **diagnosed** medical condition or disability, **and**
2. evidence that the condition is **related to your service** (service connection).

A diagnosis alone is not enough. Service connection alone is not enough.

**Gulf War Illness** is now recognized by VAC as a valid medical diagnosis. Veterans with that diagnosis are encouraged to apply.

*Source: [VAC — Disability benefits](https://veterans.gc.ca/en/financial-programs-and-services/compensation-illness-or-injury/disability-benefits)*

## The two disability-benefit schemes: Pension Act vs the Veterans Well-being Act

This is the single most confusing part of the veterans system, so it gets its own section.

### The older scheme — the Pension Act

The **Pension Act** (R.S.C., 1985, c. P-6) is the historic lifetime-pension scheme. Under it, a service-related disability is paid as a **Disability Pension**: a lifetime **monthly** payment that increases if you have dependants (spouse, common-law partner, and/or children).

The Disability Pension under the Pension Act is available if:

- you served with the CAF in the **Second World War or the Korean War**, **or**
- for other CAF service, your application for disability benefits was made **before 1 April 2006**.

*Source: [VAC — Disability benefits](https://veterans.gc.ca/en/financial-programs-and-services/compensation-illness-or-injury/disability-benefits); [Justice Laws — Pension Act](https://laws.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/P-6/)*

### The modern scheme — the Veterans Well-being Act (the "New Veterans Charter")

The benefits introduced in 2006 as the **New Veterans Charter** are now consolidated in the **Veterans Well-being Act** (cited as c-16.8 on the Justice Laws website). For most modern-era veterans (service after 2006), this is the scheme that applies.

Its main disability benefit is **Pain and Suffering Compensation (PSC)** — a tax-free payment for the impact a service-related condition has on your life. You choose between:

- a **lifetime monthly** payment, **or**
- a **lump sum** — your choice.

**Where the two schemes differ at a glance**

| | **Disability Pension (Pension Act)** | **Pain and Suffering Compensation (Veterans Well-being Act)** |
|---|---|---|
| Payment | Lifetime monthly | Monthly **or** lump sum (your choice) |
| Dependants | Increased if you have dependants | No dependant supplement |
| Who it's for | War veterans; pre-1-Apr-2006 CAF applicants | Modern-era veterans (post-2006 service) |
| Tax treatment | Tax-free | Tax-free |

Both payments are **tax-free**. The amount in either scheme depends on (a) how strongly the condition is related to service (*entitlement*) and (b) how severe it is, including its impact on quality of life (*assessment*).

*Source: [VAC — Disability benefits](https://veterans.gc.ca/en/financial-programs-and-services/compensation-illness-or-injury/disability-benefits); [Justice Laws — Veterans Well-being Act (c-16.8)](https://laws.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/c-16.8/)*

## Related programs (Veterans Well-being Act)

| Program | What it provides |
|---|---|
| **Additional Pain and Suffering Compensation** | Monthly payments for severe, permanent service-related disability that creates a barrier to life after service |
| **Critical Injury Benefit** | A **one-time** payment recognizing the immediate impact of the most severe, traumatic service-related injuries or diseases |
| **Treatment Benefits** | Coverage for medical and health-related services, prescriptions and devices |
| **Rehabilitation services** | Services to improve health and adjust to life after service |
| **Clothing allowance** | Monthly payments if you need new or special clothing because of a health issue |
| **Benefits for survivors** | Financial support or compensation for survivors of a disability pensioner, or of a member/Veteran who died in service or of a service-related illness or injury |
| **Financial advice** | Up to **$500** toward professional advice if you received a lump sum **greater than 5% of the current maximum** |
| **Telemedicine service** | Free virtual health care for Veterans **and their families** who have no primary-health-care provider |
| **Guided support** | A assigned **Veteran Service Agent** for up to 12 months to help coordinate benefits and community resources (see below) |

**Programs tied specifically to a Disability Pension (Pension Act):**

- **Attendance allowance** — monthly payments for a pensioner whose health needs require daily personal-care support
- **Exceptional incapacity allowance** — monthly payments if the illness or injury seriously affects quality of life

**Disability Pension corrective payment.** If you received a VAC benefit **between 2003 and 2010** under the Pension Act, you may be owed a corrective payment because of a calculation discrepancy. Most are automatic. Contact VAC if you no longer receive a benefit, or you represent the estate of a deceased recipient. Legally entitled inheritors can apply.

*Source: [VAC — Disability benefits](https://veterans.gc.ca/en/financial-programs-and-services/compensation-illness-or-injury/disability-benefits)*

## How VAC reviews your claim

VAC adjudicators use four main tools:

- **Eligibility Entitlement Guidelines (EEG)** — current medical and scientific descriptions of known service-related injuries and diseases.
- **Table of Disabilities** — helps assess the level of impairment and its impact on quality of life.
- **Medical questionnaires** — support diagnosis and supplement the Table of Disabilities.
- **Claim Summary Tool** — an **AI-enabled** tool VAC uses to support the review of applications and help Veterans receive decisions sooner.

*Source: [VAC — Disability benefits](https://veterans.gc.ca/en/financial-programs-and-services/compensation-illness-or-injury/disability-benefits)*

## How to apply

**Online — recommended.** Apply through **My VAC Account**, which uses a guided form (easier than the paper route).

**Mail or in person.** Download the form **PEN-923** (*Disability Benefits First Application*), then drop it at any VAC office, CAF Transition Centre, or Service Canada office, or mail it to the address on the form.

**Free help with your application:**

- staff at any VAC office, CAF Transition Centre, or Service Canada office
- **Service Officers with The Royal Canadian Legion** — free assistance
- **The War Amps of Canada** — free assistance

None of that costs you anything.

*Source: [VAC — Disability benefits](https://veterans.gc.ca/en/financial-programs-and-services/compensation-illness-or-injury/disability-benefits); [Legion — Assistance with VAC](https://www.legion.ca/support-for-veterans/assistance-with-vac)*

## The appeal process: from departmental review to VRAB

If you disagree with a VAC decision on a disability benefit, you have a right to review or appeal it. There are several stages.

### Step 1 — Departmental review (within VAC)

You may request a **departmental review in writing** and submit **new evidence** that was not in your original application. This is often the fastest fix — a common reason for denial is simply insufficient evidence the first time. A VAC adjudicator re-examines your submission and decides whether to issue a new decision.

*Source: [VAC — How to review or appeal a decision](https://veterans.gc.ca/en/veterans-rights/how-appeal)*

### Step 2 — Veterans Review and Appeal Board (VRAB)

If the departmental review does not resolve it, you can take the claim to the **Veterans Review and Appeal Board (VRAB)** — an **independent federal tribunal**, entirely separate from VAC, that provides **two levels of appeal**:

1. **Review Hearing** (first level)
   - Heard by **two** Board Members, in locations across Canada, or by video/teleconference.
   - **Non-adversarial** — no one argues against you.
   - You are encouraged to attend and testify; travel costs may be reimbursed.
   - Hearings are audio-recorded and usually last about **30 minutes**.
   - Goal: a written decision within **six weeks** of the hearing.

2. **Appeal Hearing** (second and final level)
   - Heard by **three** Board Members **not** involved in your review hearing, usually in **Charlottetown**, or by teleconference/videoconference.
   - You are **not required to attend** (the legislation does not allow oral testimony or travel reimbursement at this level).
   - The Board's decision at this level is **final and binding**.
   - Goal: a written decision within **six weeks** of the hearing.

VRAB's overall service standard is to **hear your case and issue a decision within 16 weeks** once your representative says it is ready.

**What VRAB can hear.** Pain and Suffering Compensation, Disability Pension, Disability Award, Critical Injury Benefit, special awards (Additional Pain and Suffering Compensation, Attendance Allowance, Exceptional Incapacity Allowance, Clothing Allowance), the Compassionate Award, and final appeals for the War Veterans Allowance.

**Reconsideration.** If new, credible, relevant evidence comes to light later, or an error in fact or law is found in the appeal decision, you can apply to VRAB for a reconsideration (a reopening). These are not routine.

**Judicial review.** If you remain unsatisfied, the only remaining avenue is a **judicial review at the Federal Court of Canada**. This applies only in specific circumstances (an error of law or fact, or significant overlooked facts), usually a question of interpretation of the law. **You have 30 days** to apply to the Federal Court. Lawyers from the Bureau of Pensions Advocates are **not** mandated to represent you at the Federal Court — you would need your own counsel or to represent yourself.

*Sources: [VAC — How to review or appeal a decision](https://veterans.gc.ca/en/veterans-rights/how-appeal); [VRAB — A Guide to Review and Appeal Hearings](https://vrab-tacra.gc.ca/en/what-expect/review-and-appeal-hearings/guide-review-and-appeal-hearings); [VRAB — Home](https://vrab-tacra.gc.ca/en); [Justice Laws — Veterans Review and Appeal Board Act (S.C. 1995, c. 18)](https://laws.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/v-1.6/)*

### Who can help you appeal — for free

- **Bureau of Pensions Advocates (BPA)** — a nationwide organization of lawyers **within** VAC who provide **free legal advice and representation** for disability-benefits reviews and VRAB hearings.
- **Royal Canadian Legion Service Officers** — free representation and help.
- A **private representative** of your choice, at your own expense.
- Representing yourself — permitted, but you must notify the Board in writing.

VRAB decisions are **public** (depersonalized) and published on the Canadian Legal Information Institute (CanLII) and searchable by keyword.

*Sources: [VAC — How to review or appeal a decision](https://veterans.gc.ca/en/veterans-rights/how-appeal); [VRAB — A Guide to Review and Appeal Hearings](https://vrab-tacra.gc.ca/en/what-expect/review-and-appeal-hearings/guide-review-and-appeal-hearings)*

## The Assistance Service (free, 24/7 counselling)

The **VAC Assistance Service** provides **free and confidential psychological support**, available **24 hours a day, 365 days a year**, for Veterans, former RCMP members, their family members, and caregivers. It is delivered by contracted counsellors and is separate from the disability-benefits application.

*Source: [VAC — Contact us](https://veterans.gc.ca/en/contact-us); [VAC — Talk to a mental health professional](https://veterans.gc.ca/en/contact-us/talk-mental-health-professional)*

## Career transition programs

These help you move from military to post-military working life.

### Career Transition Services (CTS)

One-on-one help with:

- career counselling
- resume writing
- interview preparation
- job searching

Delivered by VAC's national service provider (**Agilec**).

**Who qualifies:**

- a still-serving CAF member (Regular or Reserve Force) who completed basic training,
- a former member who completed basic training and was released from the CAF **on or after 1 April 2006**, or who is entitled to the Canadian Forces Income Support Benefit,
- a spouse, common-law partner, or survivor of an eligible Veteran (with the same 1 April 2006 release/survival rule).
- If you are participating in VAC's **vocational rehabilitation**, you do **not** qualify for CTS (you get career support through that service instead).

**Timing:** VAC aims to decide within **four weeks** of receiving your application. You can use CTS more than once in your lifetime. Apply through My VAC Account or by mail (form VAC-1533 for Veterans/Members; VAC-1535 for spouses/common-law/survivors).

*Source: [VAC — Career Transition Services](https://veterans.gc.ca/en/education-and-jobs/find-new-job/career-transition-services)*

### Education and Training Benefit

Financial support to help you pursue education and training that supports your career goals. Often paired with CTS (your career coach can help you choose a course of study).

*Source: [VAC — Education and Training Benefit](https://veterans.gc.ca/en/education-and-jobs/go-back-school/education-and-training-benefit)*

### Vocational Rehabilitation (for injured Veterans)

For Veterans whose injury or illness primarily resulted from service and who need help returning to work or finding new work. If you qualify for vocational rehabilitation, career-transition support comes through that service rather than CTS.

*Source: [VAC — Career Transition Services (related programs)](https://veterans.gc.ca/en/education-and-jobs/find-new-job/career-transition-services)*

## Trauma and injury benefits

| Benefit | What it covers |
|---|---|
| **Critical Injury Benefit** | A **one-time** payment recognizing the immediate impact of the **most severe, traumatic** service-related injuries or diseases |
| **Pain and Suffering Compensation / Disability Award** | Tax-free recognition for the lasting impact of a diagnosed service-related physical or mental-health condition (e.g. PTSD, hearing loss, tinnitus, Gulf War Illness) |
| **Treatment Benefits** | Medical and health-related services, prescriptions and devices |
| **Rehabilitation services** | Help to improve health and adjust to life after service |
| **VAC Assistance Service** | Free, confidential 24/7 psychological support |

## VAC vs WSIB / ODSP — why the distinction matters

| | **WSIB / provincial WC** | **VAC** |
|---|---|---|
| Trigger | Work-related injury or illness | **Service-related** injury or illness |
| Causation test | Employment + activity | Service connection + diagnosed condition |
| Appeals | Internal tribunal (WSIAT, etc.) | Departmental review → VRAB (independent tribunal) |
| Who pays | Employer-funded | Federal |

A veteran injured **at work** may have **both** a WSIB claim and a VAC claim. They are not substitutes, and a denial from one says nothing about the other.

*Sources: [VAC — Disability benefits](https://veterans.gc.ca/en/financial-programs-and-services/compensation-illness-or-injury/disability-benefits); [VAC — How to review or appeal a decision](https://veterans.gc.ca/en/veterans-rights/how-appeal)*

## Other income supports to know about

- **Canada Pension Plan (CPP) disability** and provincial disability programs (ODSP in Ontario) may apply to conditions **unrelated** to service.
- **Employment Insurance (EI)** — veterans who lose employment may qualify for regular EI like any other worker; reservists have specific statutory leave and benefit provisions. Check eligibility with the [Canada.ca EI hub](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/ei.html) or the [Benefits Finder](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/finder.html).
- **Disability Tax Credit (DTC)** — a non-refundable federal tax credit administered by the Canada Revenue Agency, not VAC. [CRA — About the DTC](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/segments/tax-credits-deductions-persons-disabilities/disability-tax-credit/about-dtc.html)
