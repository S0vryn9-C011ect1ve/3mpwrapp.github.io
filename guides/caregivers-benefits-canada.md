# Caregiver Benefits in Canada

*For the people doing the caring — spouses, adult children, parents, friends. What money and support you can claim, and how.*

## Executive Summary

Caring for someone is unpaid work that most people never claim support for — often because they don't know it exists. There are three federal dollars you may be entitled to, plus a free Ontario organization built for caregivers.

**The three federal supports:**
1. **EI caregiving benefits** — replaces part of your income when you must take time off work to care for someone critically ill/injured or needing end-of-life care. Paid by Service Canada.
2. **Canada caregiver credit** — a federal tax credit you claim when you support an infirm spouse, partner or dependant.
3. **Medical Expense Tax Credit (METC)** — you can claim the medical costs you paid for the person you care for (and yourself).

And in Ontario: the **Ontario Caregiver Organization** offers a free helpline, counselling, peer support and e-learning — no eligibility test, no cost.

**Three things to know:**
1. **For EI caregiving you do not have to be related to, or live with, the person you care for** — you only need to be "like family" and have a medical certificate.
2. **The caregiver credit and METC are claimed on your tax return**, not applied for separately. File every year.
3. **EI caregiving appeals moved** — since April 1, 2026 they go to the EI Benefits Appeal Tribunal (BOA), not the Social Security Tribunal. Deadline to challenge a decision is **30 days**.

## EI Caregiving Benefits

Employment Insurance pays **three** separate caregiving benefits. All pay **55% of your average insurable earnings, up to a maximum of $729/week (2026)**.

| Benefit | Maximum weeks | For |
|---|---|---|
| **Family caregiver benefit for children** | up to **35 weeks** | A critically ill or injured child under 18 |
| **Family caregiver benefit for adults** | up to **15 weeks** | A critically ill or injured person 18 or older |
| **Compassionate care benefit** | up to **26 weeks** | A person of any age needing end-of-life care |

**Key facts people miss:**
- You **do not need to be related** to the person, and you **do not need to live with them** — you must be considered "like family."
- The weeks can be **shared** by more than one eligible caregiver, at the same time or one after another.
- "Critically ill or injured" means a significant, acute, life-threatening change certified by a doctor or nurse practitioner. A chronic condition alone does **not** qualify unless it sharply worsens.
- **Apply** through [My Service Canada Account](https://www.canada.ca/en/employment-social-development/services/my-account.html) or a Service Canada office. You need a **medical certificate** for the person you are caring for.
- Source: [EI caregiving benefits — Canada.ca](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/ei/caregiving.html)

## Canada Caregiver Credit

A **federal non-refundable tax credit** (it reduces the tax you owe; it does not pay you a cheque directly).

- **Up to $8,601** (2025 tax year) on **line 30425** for an infirm spouse, common-law partner, or eligible dependant aged 18 or older who qualifies under line 30400.
- The amount is reduced as the dependant's net income rises, so $8,601 is the maximum at the lowest income.
- Indexed annually — confirm the current figure at the source before filing.
- Source: [Canada caregiver credit — Canada.ca](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/about-your-tax-return/tax-return/completing-a-tax-return/deductions-credits-expenses/canada-caregiver-amount.html)

## Medical Expense Tax Credit (METC)

You can claim **eligible medical expenses** you paid, including for the person you care for.

- Claim on **line 33099** (expenses for yourself, your spouse/common-law partner, and your children under 18) or **line 33199** (eligible dependants who rely on you for support).
- You can claim the **total eligible expenses above the lesser of 3% of your net income or $2,834** (2025 threshold).
- Eligible costs include attendant care, care in a facility (nursing/home/respite), prescriptions, devices, and much more. Premiums for private insurance plans (e.g., OHIP) are **not** eligible.
- The credit is non-refundable and calculated at the lowest federal tax rate.
- Source: [Eligible medical expenses (lines 33099 & 33199) — Canada.ca](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/about-your-tax-return/tax-return/completing-a-tax-return/deductions-credits-expenses/lines-33099-33199-eligible-medical-expenses-you-claim-on-your-tax-return.html)

## Ontario Caregiver Organization

The **Ontario Caregiver Organization (OCO)** is a free, provincial nonprofit for anyone caring for a family member, partner, friend or neighbour in Ontario.

- **Helpline:** 1-833-416-2273 (also live chat) — connect to support and navigation.
- **SCALE Program** — free counselling, webinars and coaching for caregiver mental health.
- **Peer support**, **e-learning** courses, and toolkits on dementia, end-of-life care, advance care planning, work/life balance, and **financial supports**.
- No income test, no cost, confidential.
- Source: [ontariocaregiver.ca](https://ontariocaregiver.ca/) (verified live 2026-10-07)

> OCO reports that over **4 million** Ontarians are caregivers and that most reach a breaking point without support — you are not alone, and the help is free.

## Appealing an EI Caregiving Decision

1. Request **reconsideration** within **30 days** of the decision (form **INS5210**). There is **no fee**.
2. Since **April 1, 2026**, EI benefits appeals go to the **EI Benefits Appeal Tribunal (BOA)** — not the Social Security Tribunal.
3. You can represent yourself or bring a trusted person.
